## The demo always looks good

You sit through a fifteen-minute pitch. The screen is clean, the presenter is smooth, and every example works perfectly. You sign up. Two weeks later the tool is still sitting open in a browser tab you haven't clicked in days.

That's not one business having a bad week. It happens across almost every small business that tries a new tool, because a demo is built to look good, not to match your actual Tuesday. And it's not free to find out the hard way. You paid for the month, someone on your team spent an afternoon learning it, and you're right back where you started, just poorer and more annoyed.

## Why "AI" isn't the real question

Search "AI tools for small business" and you'll get thousands of results, most claiming to save you hours or "transform" how you work. None of that tells you whether any single one will actually help you.

Here's a customer signal worth knowing: people who run in-person events for small business owners regularly hear that owners never walk in asking for "AI." They walk in wanting the phone answered faster, follow-up that doesn't fall through the cracks, or a proposal that doesn't eat an hour of their evening. They end up using AI tools to get those things, but AI was never the point. The task was.

That means "is this AI good?" is the wrong question. The right one is "does this fix something that's actually costing me time or money this week?"

## The three-question checklist

Before you sign up for anything, or pay for anything, run it through these three questions.

**1. Does it save real time on a task you already do?**
Not a task you might do someday. A task on your desk this week. If you can't name the task in one sentence, the tool is solving a problem you don't have yet, and you'll feel that the first time you try to explain to your team why you bought it.

**2. Can you start using it today, with what you already have?**
A real tool works with your current setup: your calendar, your inbox, your spreadsheet, your phone. If it needs a new system, a data migration, or three weeks of setup before it does anything useful, that's a project, not a quick win. Know which one you're signing up for before you commit a Tuesday to it.

**3. Can you walk away without losing anything if it doesn't work?**
Check before you sign up: is there a contract, a locked-in year, or your own customer data trapped somewhere you can't get back out? A tool that's actually good will earn your business again next month on its own. A tool that needs you locked in to keep you is telling you something about how confident it is.

If a tool passes all three, it's worth fifteen minutes to go try it. If it fails even one, you have your answer before you type in a credit card number.

## What this checklist actually protects you from

It's not really about AI. This same checklist would have caught half the software you've probably regretted buying over the years, back before anyone said "AI" out loud. The label on the tool changed. The way you should shop for it didn't.

<Takeaway>

1. Pick one repetitive task on your plate right now, something you or an employee does every week and dreads.
2. Before you pay for any tool that claims to fix it, run it through the three questions above.
3. If it fails one, walk away before you sign up, not after.

</Takeaway>

The tools actually worth your time will survive being asked plain questions. The ones that don't were counting on you not asking.
