Say you ran the five-minute test from our last post. You asked an AI tool for the best plumber in your city. It named three companies. Yours wasn't one of them.

This is a made-up example, but a lot of owners feel that drop in the stomach. You know you do good work. The AI just doesn't know it yet.

Most owners guess at the wrong fix. They rebuild the website or buy more ads. Those can help. But neither one tells an AI tool who you are.

AI tools seem to lean heavily on what other people say about your business. Your reviews. Local news. Directory listings. "Best of" lists. If that trail is thin, there isn't much for the AI to repeat.

So start with the trail. You can do one piece of it this week.

## The one fix: ask every recent customer for a review

Make a list of customers from the last few months. All of them, not just the ones who were happy with you. Send each one your review link by text or email. Ask for an honest review in their own words.

Don't suggest wording. Don't offer a reward. Google's review policy bars all three: asking only your happy customers, asking for specific content, and giving anything in exchange for a review.

Then do one more thing. Reply to each review in plain words. Name the job and the town. A reply like "Thanks, Dana. Glad we got your water heater fixed in Spokane on a Saturday" tells people what you do and where. (That reply is made up, as an example.) Keep your Google Business Profile's services and service area accurate, too.

We can't prove how much reviews move these answers, and neither can anyone selling you a guarantee. Treat this as a cheap, smart bet.

## Check again in a week

Give it seven days. Then run the same three questions from the test.

- Did your name show up?
- Did one of your new reviews get quoted?
- Did a competitor drop out?

If nothing changed, that's normal. AI answers don't shift overnight. One week of new reviews is a start.

Reviews are one piece of the trail. Your listings, your website, and what local sites say about you are the rest. Most owners have never looked at all of it in one place.
