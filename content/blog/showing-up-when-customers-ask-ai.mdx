Picture a customer with a problem. Their water heater just died, or they need a caterer for next month's wedding, or their kid needs braces. Ten years ago, they would have opened Google and scrolled through ten blue links until one looked right. Today, a lot of them skip that step. They open ChatGPT, or they just read Google's AI Overview, and type something like "best plumber in [city]" or "best wedding caterer near me."

The AI writes back two or three names.

Are you one of them?

For most small business owners, the honest answer is "I don't know." You've probably put real work into your website and your Google Business Profile. Keep doing that, it still matters. But that work was built for a different kind of search, the kind where a person reads through a list of results and picks one themselves. AI search skips the list. It reads everything it can find about your business, decides who deserves the recommendation, and hands the customer two or three names instead of ten links. If your name isn't one of them, the customer never sees your website at all. You lose the job before you even knew you were in the running.

This hits local, service-type businesses harder than most, plumbers, contractors, caterers, dentists, salons, anyone a customer picks based on trust and location. Those are exactly the searches people now hand to AI instead of digging through review sites themselves.

Here's the part that catches most owners off guard: ranking well on Google does not guarantee you show up in an AI answer. The two are related, but they are not the same test. Search engines rank pages. AI tools decide who gets **named**. A business with average search rankings but a strong trail of reviews, local press, and "best of" mentions can beat a business that has spent years on SEO but has a thin reputation everywhere else.

## The 5-minute test you can run right now

You don't need any tool for this first pass, just five minutes and your phone.

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google (Google often shows an AI Overview above the normal results) and ask it three real questions, the way an actual customer would type them:

1. "Best [your category] in [your city]"
2. "[Your category] near me for [a specific need you handle]"
3. "Is [a competitor's name] good, or is there someone better?"

Read the answers closely and ask yourself three things:

- Does it name your business at all?
- Which competitors does it name instead of you?
- Does anything in the answer sound off, like a detail about you or a competitor that isn't actually true? (AI tools do get facts wrong, and they say it with total confidence when they do.)

If you show up, good. Note where, and move on with your day. If you don't, you just found a real, specific gap in less time than it takes to make coffee.

## What to actually do with what you find

If you're invisible, the fix is rarely "spend more on ads." AI tools build their answers out of things that already exist about your business online: your reviews, mentions in local news or blogs, directory listings, and "best of" roundups. That means the fastest fix is usually about your reputation trail, not your ad budget. If your last real review is a year old, that's a bigger problem for AI visibility than a slightly outdated website. Pick the one gap that's easiest to close first, not five things at once.

Run the 5-minute test today. The customer asking AI for a recommendation right now isn't going to wait around for you to catch up.
